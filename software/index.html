graph-theory-AI

graph-theory-AI is an open organisation, started in 2026, that applies large language models to open problems in graph theory. Its main projects are:

  • Mathpocalypse. A study testing whether an open-weight language model, run only on public research computers, can find genuine errors in published mathematics. The model reread hundreds of papers in graph theory and combinatorics on a national supercomputer and flagged steps that may be wrong; each serious flag was re-checked before the authors were contacted. Several research groups have updated their arXiv papers to acknowledge the project. DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21499917

  • Graph Conjectures. A large, browsable collection of over a thousand problems and conjectures in graph theory, gathered from Open Problem Garden, recent arXiv papers and Bondy and Murty's textbook.

  • Graph Theory LLM Proofs. Frontier models attacking the open problems collected in Graph Conjectures. Several of their proofs, read and confirmed by mathematicians and often also machine-checked in Rocq, settle conjectures and questions posed by leading graph theorists such as Aharoni, Alon and Chudnovsky, or Scott and Seymour; dozens more results, including on older problems from Open Problem Garden, are awaiting human review.

Leanamics

Leanamics is a Lean 4 library of formally verified results on opinion dynamics and related processes: rumor spreading, voter and Moran processes, epidemics, averaging, etc. AI agents formalize the statements and the published proofs from the original papers, and Lean checks every step; a blueprint links each formal result to the paper proof it comes from. The project is at an early stage, and contributions are welcome through its roadmap.

Older projects

  • WorldDynamics.jl (co-lead) (2021–2024). An open-source Julia framework for global integrated assessment models, which reimplements the World1–World3 models in a modular way (JOSS 2024). We built on it the Earth4All.jl implementation of the Earth for All model, and used it for a sensitivity analysis of that model (JIE 2024).

  • KADABRA (co-developer) (2016). An adaptive sampling algorithm for approximating betweenness centrality in large networks (ESA 2016, JEA 2019), with Michele Borassi. It is included in NetworKit since version 5.0.
